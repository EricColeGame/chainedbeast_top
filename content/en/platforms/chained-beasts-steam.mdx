## What Is Chained Beasts on Steam?

### How a Run Actually Works

Structurally, Chained Beasts is a roguelite. Each day in the arena ends with new wounds layered onto your gladiator — a limp, a missing left eye, hallucinations only you can see. Potions dull the pain but can stack worse side effects, and surgery is presented as a risky alternative. Between runs you build up crowd favor, spend coin on gear and weapons, and unlock new characters with distinct abilities, so a failed run still moves the needle.

The community-applied tags tell the story pretty well: Co-op, Action Roguelike, Hack and Slash, Top-Down, PvE, Roguelite, Funny, Medieval, Perma Death, Swordplay, Difficult, and 4 Player Local. If you like the idea of a slapstick combat game with real punishment for sloppy teamwork, this sits squarely in that lane. ## Tips for Surviving the Arena

The first few hours of Chained Beasts are where most groups bounce off. The chain punishes panic, and the arena punishes bad positioning. These habits will get you further than raw aggression.

- **Treat the chain as a weapon, not a leash.** Synchronized movement lets you trip enemies and yank teammates out of pits. Practice coordinated pulls in early, low-stakes waves.
- **Assign roles out loud.** One player calls hazards, one calls targets. Silent groups end up wrapped around pillars.
- **Use the arena before you use your sword.** Urns, spike walls, and snake pits do the work for you — and enemies can be thrown into all three.
- **Don't hoard thrown weapons.** Ranged throws are strong, but running dry mid-fight is worse than an early throw. Keep a melee option in reserve.
- **Manage wounds deliberately.** Potions relieve pain but can add new afflictions, and surgery is the high-risk alternative. Read the trade-off before you drink.
- **Bank crowd favor and coin.** Winning over the crowd pays out, and equipment and character unlocks carry your progression between runs.
