## Gameplay: What You Actually Do in the Arena

The hook is simple and slightly absurd: you and up to three friends are chained together, and the chains are a weapon as much as a handicap. Coordinate and you can trip enemies, swing each other into crowds, or drag a teammate out of a spike wall. Fail to coordinate and you'll wrap yourselves around a pillar while a boss closes in.

Combat leans on the environment as much as your blade. You can hurl urns at foes, hurl foes at urns, charge enemies into spike walls or snake pits, and throw weapons for ranged kills — with the caveat that you'd better have a backup in hand. Between fights, the roguelike layer does the heavy lifting:

- **Persistent injuries** carry between days in the arena. A limp, a missing eye, or hallucinations that only you can see all change how a run plays.
- **Potions and surgery** offer relief, but side effects can leave you worse off than the original wound.
- **Crowd favor** is a resource. Win over the bloodthirsty spectators and they'll back you when it counts.
- **Meta progression** unlocks new characters, skills, and equipment purchased with arena earnings.

Performance-wise, the requirements are modest. You'll want a 64-bit OS, 8 GB of RAM, and a GPU in the GTX 1050 / RX 460 range on Windows, or an Apple M1-class chip on macOS.
