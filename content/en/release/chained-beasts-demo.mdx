## What the Demo Reveals About Combat, Chains, and Injuries

Even a short session exposes the systems that define the full game. Here is what to watch for while you play.

### The chain is a weapon, not a leash

Your tether is the whole design. Swing it, yank a teammate into position, or use the momentum to trip an opponent. In the full release, uncoordinated groups get tangled around pillars; synchronized groups turn the tether into a tool. Test both on purpose — deliberately play messy once, then play tight, and you will feel the difference immediately.

### The environment does the heavy lifting

Combat rewards improvisation. Urns can be hurled at enemies, enemies can be hurled at urns, and both can be introduced to spike walls or snake pits. Thrown weapons work at range, but you will want a backup in hand — running dry mid-fight is a common early mistake.

### Injuries persist and stack

Each day in the arena leaves a mark. A limp, a missing eye, or hallucinations only your character can see are all part of the progression loop. Potions dull the pain but can inflict worse side effects, and surgery is presented as a riskier alternative. This is the system most likely to surprise new players, because it turns survival into a resource-management problem rather than a pure skill check.

### Bosses escalate with your fame

As your reputation grows, arena champions show up armed with every advantage. The trial is a reasonable place to gauge whether that difficulty curve suits you — the game is tagged both "Souls-like" and "Difficult" by its own community.

Steam Deck compatibility is currently listed as unknown, so handheld players should test the demo on Deck before committing — that is one of the quiet advantages of a free build.

A few habits that make a first session go better:

- **Assign roles out loud.** One player anchors, one flanks, one handles projectiles. Groups that improvise silently tend to knot themselves around scenery.
- **Learn the throw button early.** Ranged weapon throws save runs, but only if you remember to pick up a replacement.
- **Do not hoard potions.** Side effects are part of the design; a bad affliction now beats a death later.
- **Bank crowd favor.** Winning over the audience is framed as a long-term advantage, not a cosmetic flourish.
- **Play one solo run.** The game supports single-player, and a solo session teaches you the tether physics without four voices shouting over each other.

Yes — with a caveat about expectations. The caveat: do not judge the game on a solo, keyboard-only, ten-minute poke. This is a game about bodies colliding, chains snagging, and friends yelling at each other in a good way. Load it up with the people you would actually play with, plug in controllers, and give it an honest hour. If the tether physics make you laugh rather than groan, the full release is an easy call.
