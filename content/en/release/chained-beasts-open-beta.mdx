## Co-op Tips From Players Already in the Pit

Community reports from the first week paint a consistent picture: the chains are a weapon, not a leash. Players who treat the tether as a tool — swinging a partner into an enemy, dragging a teammate out of a spike wall, using tension to trip a charging foe — consistently outperform groups that fight as four individuals. Communication matters more than reflexes.

Environment kills are the great equalizer. Urns, spike walls, snake pits, and ledges do more damage than most early weapons, and throwing an enemy is often faster than cutting them down. Thrown weapons are strong but leave you empty-handed, so keep a backup. And when it comes to wounds, the arena's potions are a gamble — the side effects can be worse than the ailment, so weigh surgery against the limp.

- Prioritize crowd favor early; it pays out over the course of a run.
- Bank coin for equipment that patches a weakness rather than a marginal damage bump.
- Test new characters on easier days, not before a boss.
- Expect friendly fire. Laugh, then reposition.

Partially. Remote Play Together lets a single owner stream local co-op to friends online, and the game also supports online co-op, split screen, and cross-platform multiplayer for everyone who owns a copy.

The standard price is $19.99, with the introductory 35% discount bringing it to $12.99 during the launch window. Prices vary by region and promotions rotate, so check the store listing for the current figure.

Both are viable, but the design leans co-op. Solo runs are cleaner and more tactical; group runs are chaotic, funnier, and considerably harder to coordinate — which is precisely the point of the chains.
