Your first Chained Beasts run can end in seconds, because the chain that saves you is also the chain that drags you into a spike wall. The tether links every gladiator in your party, so a teammate's retreat changes your reach, your throwing angle, and your escape route. This chained beasts beginner guide focuses on the three decisions that shape early runs: where the chain sits, what you throw, and which affliction you accept for healing.

Treat this chained beasts beginner guide as a checklist rather than a lore tour. You do not need perfect inputs to survive Carthage — you need a reachable replacement weapon and a healing penalty that leaves your main attack usable.

## Chain Positioning: The Core Skill in Chained Beasts

In co-op, moving your gladiator changes the space available to anyone attached to the same chain. A teammate retreating behind a pillar can pull the tether straight across your route. While you are learning a new fight, keep the group in one open part of the arena with the chain visible between players. The first lesson of any chained beasts beginner guide is that positioning is a shared resource, not a personal one.

### Setting Up a Chain Trip

A chain trip puts an enemy on the floor while your partner still has a weapon ready to finish the fight. Run the drill in an open stretch with no pillar between the two gladiators.

| Step | Who moves | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Split | Both gladiators flank the enemy's approach | Keep the chain visible between players |
| 2. Anchor | One gladiator holds position | Anchor keeps a weapon drawn and ready |
| 3. Sweep | The other steps sideways | The tether crosses the enemy's path |
| 4. Finish | Both attack the downed enemy | The knockdown confirms the chain connected |

This arrangement lets the anchor stay armed while the moving player creates the opening. More players make the chain's route harder to track, so call the intended direction before crossing a teammate's position. Community reports from early co-op sessions agree that most failed trips come from unannounced movement, not from bad timing.

### Pillars and Wrapped Links

Pillars need a different approach because the tether can wrap around them. If the chain catches, retrace the movement that wrapped it until the links clear the obstacle. Keep the other attached gladiator near the pillar while unwinding — pulling farther away keeps tension on the obstruction and makes the wrap worse.

## Throwing Weapons Without Getting Caught Empty-Handed

Thrown weapons hit enemies outside melee range, but you give up the weapon in your hand. Before a throw, identify a replacement on the arena floor and a route your current chain position actually lets you reach. A spare behind your partner or around a pillar may be inaccessible when the next enemy closes in. Many chained beasts beginner guide mistakes begin with a throw you cannot recover from.

| Check before throwing | Question to ask | Fix if the answer is no |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replacement | Is a weapon inside my reachable area? | Move along the tether first |
| Route | Can I reach it without crossing a pillar? | Reposition before throwing |
| Hazard | Does the pickup path cross a pit or spike wall? | Approach from open ground |
| Cover | Is a partner free to screen the pickup? | Delay the throw |

Urns provide another ranged attack, and you can throw enemies into objects or charge them toward spike walls and snake pits. Approach a hazard from an angle that keeps your own retreat on open ground. When several gladiators share the tether, a teammate's route toward a dropped sword can pull the whole group toward the same hazard.

The opening tutorial includes blocking and throwing practice. Finish the throw-and-block exercise with the partner's shield before starting an arena run. It teaches input timing without adding a snake pit or a pillar to the exchange. ## Scorecards, Executions, and Crowd Conditions

The post-battle scorecard separates Variety, Tempo, and Carnage, and it also shows health lost plus the Crowd Favorite banner. Use those named rows to pick one specific change for the next fight. A low Variety result is a clear reason to add a weapon throw or an environmental attack to your melee routine. This is the section of a chained beasts beginner guide that most players skip, and it is the cheapest way to improve.

| Scorecard row | What it tracks | Beginner response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variety | Mix of attack types | Add a throw or hazard kill |
| Tempo | Pace of the fight | Cut downtime, engage sooner |
| Carnage | Damage dealt | Commit to executions after trips |
| Health lost | Damage taken | Fix positioning before gear |
| Crowd Favorite | Crowd approval | Check crowd conditions for skills |

Executions finish downed enemies, so a chain trip creates both a damage opening and an execution opportunity — provided nearby enemies leave room for the animation. Let a partner cover the approach when another opponent is still close enough to interrupt. Coins earned after each fight fund purchases between arenas, which makes a clean execution worth more than a flashy one.

Crowd conditions also affect some skills. On a Roll grants four seconds of invulnerability after a kill while the crowd is going wild, according to its displayed skill card. Take it when you can reliably produce kills during that crowd state; a defensive plan that depends on the effect needs both conditions active at once.

## Healing Potions and Afflictions

Healing potions carry afflictions into later fights. The health gain can keep a run alive, while the attached penalty changes movement, vision, or the decisions available between arenas. A chained beasts beginner guide that treats potions as free health will leave you steering a build you cannot control.

| Affliction | Effect | Adjustment for the next fight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annoying | Allies often attack when you get too close | Leave space around the affected gladiator when arranging the chain |
| Impulsive | Forces a new skill choice every second level and an item purchase at every shop | Budget for forced spending before saving toward another purchase |
| Infatuated | Camera follows a particular ally; 10% extra damage when that ally looks at you | Keep the followed ally near the fight so the camera covers your combat area |

Annoying makes close formations harder because the group already shares limited movement through the tether. Give the affected gladiator a clear side of the fight; a partner drifting into that space to grab a weapon can trigger attacks while enemies are still approaching.

Impulsive hits progression and money at the same time. Its forced purchase can break a savings plan, so compare your balance after every shop before committing to another item. The forced skill selection every second level also limits how long you can wait for a preferred combination.

Infatuated is the friendliest of the three for aggressive players. Fighting near the followed ally keeps your gladiator in view and makes the 10% damage bonus easier to hold.

## Night Purchases, Trainer Cards, and Goliath

Between-fight menus let you learn skills and buy equipment. Choose upgrades for your preferred attacks, and pair throw-focused skills with positioning that keeps replacements within reach. Trainer card offers vary, so Strong Arm and Concussive Strength may be absent on your first night. Consider your current afflictions too: a penalty that reduces throwing distance changes the practical reach of a throw build even when Strong Arm raises its damage.

| Situation | Priority purchase | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throws are your main damage | Throw-scaling skills | Improves the ranged opener you already use |
| Affliction shortened your range | Surgeon fix for movement or vision | Restores the action your build depends on |
| Dying before the crowd heats up | Defensive skill such as On a Roll | Four seconds of safety after a kill while the crowd is wild |
| Impulsive forcing a purchase | Cheap item early | Protects your savings plan |

The Surgeon appears between acts and can manage afflictions or offer body augmentations that grant skills. Each procedure has a cost, so prioritize an effect that restores something your build needs — useful movement or visibility — when an affliction has made that action difficult. This chained beasts beginner guide recommends fixing a build-breaking penalty before buying a second damage upgrade.

## FAQ

### How do I trip an enemy with the chain in Chained Beasts?

Position one gladiator on each side of the enemy's path, hold one in place as the anchor, then step the other sideways so the tether sweeps across the approach. Attack the downed enemy to confirm the connection. Practice in an open stretch with no pillar between players.

### Should I throw my weapon in Chained Beasts?

Only when a replacement is already inside your reachable area. Throwing gives up the weapon in your hand, and a spare behind a partner or around a pillar may be unreachable when the next enemy closes in. Throw, then pick up before returning to melee.

### Which healing affliction is safest for a new player?

It depends on your build. Annoying punishes tight formations, Impulsive forces spending and skill choices, and Infatuated moves the camera while granting 10% extra damage when the followed ally faces you. Pick the penalty that interferes least with your main attack, then plan a Surgeon visit.

### What is the best chained beasts beginner guide tip for Goliath?

Clear the tether before you approach. Goliath's jump and ground slam punish cramped space, so leave an open escape route in Germania, especially when a pillar limits how far the chain can travel.
