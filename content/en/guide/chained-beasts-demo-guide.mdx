## Co-op Setup: Roles, Communication, and Chain Awareness

Before the gate opens, agree on a simple plan. A practical Chained Beasts demo guide starts with roles, not builds. You do not need formal classes; you need to know who is anchoring space, who is applying pressure, and who is watching the chain for tangles.

For this Chained Beasts demo guide, the useful split is anchor, striker, support, and flex. In a two-player group, one player can anchor while the other strikes, then swap after each wave. In a four-player group, the flex player should avoid becoming a second anchor by default.

| Suggested role | Main job | Chain habit |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Holds space and controls pace | Keeps slack manageable and calls pulls |
| Striker | Applies damage and finishes targets | Rotates around the anchor instead of crossing the chain |
| Support | Tracks injuries and spots hazards | Stays between the rescuer and the exit |
| Flex | Fills gaps and handles objectives | Mirrors the partner and avoids double commits |

Communication should be short. Use callouts like “pull left,” “hold here,” and “reset.” If a partner is injured, shorten the fight rather than chasing a flashy finish. The chain rewards players who move with intent and punishes panic.

## Chain Tactics and Arena Positioning That Work

The chain is not just a handicap. The official description says players can use their chains or the arena to beat up foes, so look for moments when the link can sweep, trip, or redirect enemies. The chain section of any Chained Beasts demo guide would be incomplete without one warning: long, loose loops create tripwires for your own team.

| Situation | Chain tactic | Arena tactic | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enemies clustered | Sweep the chain through the group while a partner anchors | Back toward a wall to limit flanking | Both players charging the same target |
| Partner injured | Close distance and screen the retreat | Use pillars or barriers to break line of sight | Dragging the partner into open ground |
| Tougher enemy | Keep the chain on one side and rotate together | Force the enemy toward a hazard | Circling in opposite directions |
| Small enemies | Use the chain to separate or trip foes | Kite around obstacles | Panic pulling from maximum range |
| Rescue moment | Anchor outside danger and pull in short bursts | Clear the escape path first | Yanking a partner through a crowd |

Positioning matters as much as damage. Fight with a wall or barrier at your back when you can, and keep the chain on one side of the pair instead of crossing it through the middle. If one player circles left while the other circles right, the chain becomes a closing trap for both of you. Rotate together, even when the target is almost dead.

## Injury Management and Demo Run Planning

The demo trailer highlights injury management, and that fits the roguelike structure. Damage is not just a health bar; it is a signal to change your plan. This Chained Beasts demo guide recommends treating injuries as a reason to shorten fights, not as a reason to take more risks for a quick kill.

| Priority | Warning sign | Best response | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green | Minor chip damage | Keep fighting but tighten spacing | Ignoring positioning |
| Yellow | One player is low or limping | Shorten the fight and rotate the anchor | Letting the injury snowball |
| Red | Chain is dragging or a rescue is needed | Disengage, clear a path, and reset | Greedy finishing attempts |
| Critical | Both players are near defeat | Abandon the objective and survive | Losing the run to pride |

Run planning matters too. Set one learning goal per run: practice chain pulls, test a wall-heavy arena, or learn how early you need to disengage. End a run early if you are testing a tactic; you will learn more from a clean experiment than from a desperate clear.

- Rotate who anchors between runs.
- Test one chain tactic at a time.
- Note which arena layouts punish long chains.
- Practice retreating before injuries become critical.
- Do not judge the demo from one chaotic four-player match.

Rotate who anchors between runs. The player who always anchors may learn spacing, but they may not learn how to escape when the chain is under pressure. Swap roles while the stakes are low.

## Common Mistakes, Player Feedback, and Next Steps

Most demo mistakes come from treating the chain as cosmetic. Players spread apart, both commit to the same target, ignore arena hazards, or hide injuries until someone is critical. The fix is boring but effective: move as a pair, leave an exit, and call out danger before it becomes a rescue.

- Treating the chain as decoration instead of a tool.
- Overcommitting both players to the same enemy.
- Fighting in open ground when a wall is available.
- Waiting until a partner is critical before calling for help.
- Trying four-player chaos before learning two-player spacing.

## FAQ: Chained Beasts Demo Guide

### Is the Chained Beasts demo multiplayer?

Chained Beasts is designed as a 1–4 player co-op game, so the demo is best tested with at least one friend. Solo play can teach you chain spacing, but the real tension comes from coordinating two or more players.

### How many players should I bring to the demo?

Start with two. A pair makes it easier to learn callouts and chain spacing without the chaos of a full group. Move to three or four once you can move together without tangling.

### What is the best way to learn the chain mechanic?

Pick one tactic per run. Practice short pulls, wall-side positioning, or rescuing an injured partner. This Chained Beasts demo guide recommends avoiding long, loose loops until you can keep the chain on one side of the fight.

### Is the Chained Beasts demo worth trying solo?

Yes, if you want to learn arena layouts and basic timing. But the game’s identity is co-op, so treat solo as practice rather than the main event.
