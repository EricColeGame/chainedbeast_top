Choosing a role in Featherweight Games' co-op gladiator roguelike is less about locking into a rigid archetype and more about surviving the arena as one unit. Understanding Chained Beasts classes matters because your character, your gear, and your chains form a single machine — and a mismatched squad falls apart fast in the Colosseum. This guide breaks down how Chained Beasts classes actually work, what each role brings to a chain gang, and how to build a lineup that survives bosses, ghastly wounds, and the crowd's endless demands.

## How Classes Work in Chained Beasts

Unlike a traditional RPG where you pick a class from a menu and follow a fixed skill tree, Chained Beasts treats character identity as something you assemble across runs. The [official Chained Beasts page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3569420/Chained_Beasts/) describes the loop directly: you learn powerful skills, win over the crowd, save money for equipment and weapons, and unlock new characters with powerful abilities. A "class" here is really three layers stacked together — the character you bring, the skills you find mid-run, and the gear you can afford before the next fight.

That structure matters because the game is a roguelike with permadeath. Your run ends, your injuries pile up, and you start again — but your unlocks and your understanding of the Chained Beasts classes carry forward. Players who treat a run as a fixed build usually stall out; players who adapt to what the arena offers tend to climb faster.

| Traditional RPG Class System | How Chained Beasts Handles It |
|---|---|
| Class locked in at character creation | Characters unlocked over time through play |
| Fixed skill tree per class | Skills learned during a run, plus crowd favor |
| Class-restricted gear | Weapons and equipment purchased in the arena |
| Death means a reload | Permadeath resets the run, not your unlocks |
| Identity is chosen | Identity is built from character + skills + gear |

Because each run pushes you down a different path, the Chained Beasts classes you end up playing can look very different from the ones you planned. Flexibility beats loyalty to a single character.

## The Core Role Archetypes in Chained Beasts Classes

The game doesn't publish a rigid class roster with named specializations, so the community tends to talk in roles instead. Based on player experience since the October 15, 2026 launch, four broad archetypes show up again and again in successful squads.

| Role | What It Does | Chain Consideration | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontliner | Starts fights, pressures enemies, absorbs attention | Needs a partner who won't yank the chain mid-swing | Aggressive players who like reading enemy tells |
| Anchor | Holds ground, controls space, stabilizes the chain | Naturally becomes the pivot point of the formation | Patient players who enjoy positioning |
| Chaos-Maker | Uses the arena — urns, spike walls, snake pits — to kill | Ranges wide, so chain length becomes a leash | Creative players who love environmental kills |
| Quartermaster | Manages money, potions, crowd favor, and recovery | Stays central so the squad can regroup on them | Support-minded players who track resources |

A few notes on how these play out in practice:

- **Frontliners** set the tempo. They decide when a fight starts, which means they also decide when the chain gets dragged into danger.
- **Anchors** are the reason a tangled squad doesn't panic. When you're wrapped around a pillar, someone has to stay calm and call the direction.
- **Chaos-Makers** convert the environment into damage. Hurling a foe into a pit of snakes is often faster than any combo.
- **Quartermasters** win the long game. Crowd favor and potions decide whether day three is survivable.

The important thing is that a role is never purely personal — it's positional. Your archetype only works if the chain allows you to stand where that archetype needs to stand.

## Building a Chain Gang: Team Composition by Player Count

Chained Beasts supports one to four players, with online co-op, shared/split-screen, cross-platform multiplayer, and Remote Play Together. The size of your squad changes which Chained Beasts classes are worth bringing.

| Squad Size | Recommended Mix | Chain Reality | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | Hybrid Frontliner/Quartermaster | No chain partner, so positioning is entirely on you | Survivability and crowd favor |
| Duo | Frontliner + Anchor | Short chain, easy to coordinate, low tangle risk | Boss preparation |
| Trio | Frontliner + Chaos-Maker + Quartermaster | Medium chain, needs one designated caller | Resource management |
| Full squad of four | Two Frontliners, one Anchor, one Quartermaster | Long chain, high tangle risk, huge payoff | Communication discipline |

Two practical notes from the game's own setup: the developers recommend a controller, and same-screen co-op requires at least one gamepad. If you're playing locally with a full squad, plan your seating — literally. Players who can see each other's screens coordinate chain movement far better than players shouting over voice chat.

For duos, the Frontliner-plus-Anchor pairing is the most forgiving entry point. For four-player groups, the temptation is to bring four damage dealers. Player experience suggests that fails more often than it succeeds, because nobody is left to manage potions, money, and crowd favor when the wounds start stacking.

## How the Chain Mechanic Changes Class Value

The chain isn't a cosmetic gimmick — it's the core constraint that determines which Chained Beasts classes are actually strong. The store page puts it well: if you can't coordinate, you end up tangled around a pillar or worse, but synchronized effort lets you trip enemies and turn the tables.

| Chain Situation | Role That Shines | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Wrapped around a pillar | Anchor | Stop moving, call a direction, let the chain unwind |
| Enemy clustered near a hazard | Chaos-Maker | Drag the chain toward the spike wall or snake pit |
| Boss winding up a heavy attack | Frontliner | Bait the swing while the Anchor holds the line |
| Squad low on health mid-fight | Quartermaster | Pull everyone toward the exit lane and burn a potion |
| Chain too long in a crowded room | Any | Shorten formation before the fight, not during it |

The counterintuitive lesson: damage output matters less than chain discipline. A squad of moderately geared players who move as a unit will outlast a squad of high-damage players who fight the chain. Community reports consistently describe the same failure state — one player charging while three players are still recovering — and the same fix: assign a caller before you enter the arena.

## Progression, Wounds, and Unlocking New Characters

Chained Beasts doesn't just reward you with power; it also punishes you with permanence. Each day in the arena leaves new wounds, and those wounds directly change which roles you can play effectively.

| Affliction | Effect on Your Role | How to Adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Painful limp | Reduced mobility, harder to reposition | Lean into Anchor duty; let faster teammates scout |
| Missing left eye | Impaired awareness on one side | Stay central in the formation, not on a flank |
| Hallucinations | Unreliable reads on threats | Play safer roles; rely on the caller's information |
| Stacked potion side effects | Compounding penalties | Consider surgery, or accept a slower, safer run |

Potions relieve pain but can leave you with worse afflictions, and surgery is presented as the heavier option. That's the trade-off that makes Chained Beasts classes feel like a real decision rather than a costume change — you're not just choosing what you can do, you're choosing what you can survive.

A few actionable habits that make progression smoother:

- **Bank crowd favor early.** Winning the crowd gives you a safety net that persists into harder fights.
- **Buy weapons before armor.** A thrown weapon solves problems that positioning can't.
- **Unlock characters deliberately.** New characters bring powerful abilities, so treat unlocks as long-term investments rather than side content.
- **Retire a run that's bleeding.** A squad carrying three compounding wounds usually loses more than it gains.

For context on how the game is landing with players: at the time of writing, the Steam page shows a "Very Positive" rating with 83% of 655 reviews positive, and the developers posted a launch update noting 60,000 gladiators had entered the arena. That's a healthy signal that the class and role system is readable enough for newcomers to stick with.

## FAQ

**How many Chained Beasts classes are there?**
The game doesn't use a fixed, numbered class list in the traditional RPG sense. Instead, you unlock new characters with powerful abilities over time, then layer run-specific skills and purchased gear on top. Think of Chained Beasts classes as combinations rather than locked loadouts — check your in-game roster for what you've unlocked so far.

**Can you play Chained Beasts classes solo?**
Yes. The game supports one to four players, and solo runs are viable — you simply lose the chain partner that defines co-op positioning. Solo players should favor self-sufficient builds with strong crowd favor and reliable recovery.

**Do character unlocks carry between runs?**
Chained Beasts is a roguelike with permadeath, so the run itself resets when you fall. Broader progression — including unlocked characters — is what makes each attempt easier than the last. In-run skills and equipment are part of the run, not permanent power.

**Which Chained Beasts classes are best for beginners?**
Start with an Anchor-style character or a Quartermaster build. Both reward positioning and resource management over twitch reflexes, and both stay useful even when your chain gets tangled. Once you're comfortable reading the arena, move to a Frontliner or Chaos-Maker for higher damage ceilings.
