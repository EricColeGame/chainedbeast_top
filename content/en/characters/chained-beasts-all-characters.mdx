## How Character Unlocks Actually Work

Nobody unlocks the full roster in a weekend. Chained Beasts gates characters behind the same currency and reputation systems that drive everything else, so progress is cumulative rather than luck-based. According to player experience and community reports, the reliable unlock engines are the ones you'd expect from a roguelike of this shape.

| Unlock Trigger | Where to Track It | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| Crowd favor | In-run crowd meter | Play to the audience — environmental kills and flashy finishers earn more than safe chip damage |
| Coin and purchases | Between-run shop | Bank a portion every run instead of spending it all on consumables |
| Boss clears | Run summary screen | Push deeper before farming; later bosses gate more content than early repeats |
| Achievements | Steam achievement list (60 total) | Hunt achievements deliberately — they double as a content checklist |
| Milestone progression | Post-launch patch notes | Read the updates; the studio has committed to progression improvements |

The crowd is the piece most new players undervalue. The game is explicit that winning over the audience pays you back, and in practice that means favor compounds across a run. A team that plays safe and boring will out-survive a flashy team early, then fall behind once the flashy team's favor turns into gear.

> **Player experience note:** Community reports consistently describe the unlock curve as front-loaded — the first several gladiators come quickly, then the pace slows hard. Don't assume you're doing something wrong when unlocks stall around the mid-game.

## Reading a Character Kit Before You Commit

Before you spend a run on a fresh unlock, run it through the same five questions. This is the checklist chained beasts all characters discussions keep circling back to, just formalized.

| Question | Strong Answer | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Does the ability work alone or only with a teammate? | Useful solo, better in a pair | Dead weight in single-player |
| Can it break a chain tangle? | Has an escape or repositioning tool | Relies on teammates to untangle it |

| Does it earn crowd favor? | Rewards dramatic plays | Only efficient, never impressive |

A kit that fails two or more of these is a kit you take into a run you're already planning to lose — which is fine, sometimes, but don't confuse it with a main.

### The solo versus co-op split

Chained Beasts supports 1–4 players, and the same character can feel completely different depending on how many chains are attached to it. In solo play your ability has to carry the whole fight. In a four-player chain, a utility kit that trips enemies or controls space is worth more than a second damage dealer, because the damage is already there.

Community reports suggest most players over-invest in damage early and under-invest in anything that manages positioning. Given that the arena itself is a weapon — urns, spike walls, snake pits — a kit that moves enemies is effectively a damage kit.

## Building a Team Around What You Own

Team composition in Chained Beasts isn't about stacking the strongest characters. It's about covering the three jobs nobody wants to do: controlling space, absorbing pressure, and finishing bosses.

| Team Size | Composition Priority | What To Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Solo | Self-sufficient kit with an escape and crowd control | Glass cannons with no way out of a tangle |
| Two players | One space controller + one burst finisher | Two identical kits that share the same weakness |
| Three players | Add a durable front-liner who can eat wounds | Three squishy kits chained together |
| Four players | One of each role, plus a utility pick for chain management | Everyone chasing the same damage build |

A useful rule of thumb: if your team can't answer "who untangles us when we wrap a pillar?" you've already lost a run to that question. Assign it out loud before you enter the arena.

### Chain discipline is a character skill too

The chain isn't a debuff — it's a shared tool. Coordinated teams use it to trip enemies and turn the tables, while uncoordinated teams end up wrapped around architecture. Whichever gladiator is holding the shortest link should be the one calling pulls. That's a role, not a stat.

## Progression: Skills, Injuries, and Equipment

Your character isn't static across a run. Skills, gear, and the wounds you accumulate all change what your gladiator is good at by the time you reach a boss.

| Progression Layer | What It Changes | Practical Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Learned skills | Adds tools to your kit | Take skills that patch your worst matchup, not your best one |
| Purchased equipment | Weapons and armor from saved coin | Buy one run-defining item instead of three small ones |
| Wounds | Persistent penalties across the day | A limp or a lost eye changes your role — lean into the new one |
| Potions | Temporary relief with side effects | Read the side effect before drinking; some are worse than the wound |
| Surgery | The risky fix for stacked afflictions | Save it for a wound that's actively breaking your build |

The wound system is where the roster gets interesting. A character who relies on mobility becomes a different pick entirely once a painful limp sets in, while a stationary bruiser barely notices. Before you blame a character for feeling weak, check whether you're actually playing a wounded version of them.

## Five Mistakes Players Make With the Roster

- **Judging a gladiator on one bad run.** A kit that got dragged into a snake pit on minute two never showed you what it does.
- **Ignoring crowd favor.** It's not flavor — it's an unlock engine and a mid-run power source.
- **Running duplicate kits in co-op.** Two identical gladiators share the same blind spot, and the arena finds it fast.
- **Skipping the demo.** A free demo is available on Steam; test a playstyle before you commit to a character grind.
- **Treating the roster as a tier list.** Balance changes are ongoing — the studio has publicly committed to further tuning, so today's weak pick may not stay weak.
