Four gladiators, one couch, and a single shared set of chains — that is the entire pitch of **Chained Beasts local coop**, and it is why the game has become a living-room staple since it launched on Steam in October 2026. Fighting through a brutal arena roguelike is tense alone. Fighting it shoulder to shoulder with three friends, shouting over every stumble and every badly timed dodge, is something else entirely.

Chained Beasts local coop matters because coordination is not a bonus here — it is the core mechanic. Your characters are physically linked, so every sprint, swing, and panicked roll drags someone else along with you. When everyone is in the same room, you can see the tangle forming, laugh at it, and correct it in real time. Online play works fine, but couch play is where the chaos gets loudest.

## Chained Beasts Local Coop Setup: What You Actually Need

The good news is that couch play is officially supported. The less good news is that a four-player session takes a little planning, because a shared screen plus four people plus a chain that yanks everyone around is a lot to manage on a small TV.

| Way to play | How it works | What you need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared/split-screen co-op | Everyone plays on one machine and one display | One gaming PC or Mac, controllers, a large screen | Same-room parties and chaos |
| Remote Play Together | A host runs a local session and invites friends over the internet | Host owns the game; guests join through Steam | Friends who live far apart but want the couch feel |
| Online co-op | Standard online lobbies | A copy of the game and an internet connection | Scheduled sessions with a regular group |
| Cross-platform multiplayer | Play with friends on other platforms | Supported platforms and accounts | Mixed friend groups |

A few practical notes before you sit down:

- **Plan for one controller per player.** The Steam listing notes that at least one gamepad is required for same-screen co-op, and the developers recommend controllers generally. Four pads removes the awkward "who gets the keyboard" conversation.
- **Test the setup before guests arrive.** Connect pads, confirm each one registers, and load into a quick run solo so you are not troubleshooting drivers while three people wait.
- **Sit close to the screen.** With a shared view and four characters bunched on a chain, details like a spike wall or a snake pit are easy to miss from across the room.
- **Give one person the "caller" job.** More on that below, but a single voice directing movement prevents a lot of chain-related disasters.

## How to Start a Chained Beasts Local Coop Run

The flow is straightforward once you know the order. Exact menu labels may shift with patches, so treat these as the general steps rather than a script.

1. **Connect every controller first.** Pair pads before launching the game so the game detects them cleanly at startup.
2. **Launch the game and open the multiplayer or co-op menu.** Look for the shared-screen or local co-op option rather than online.
3. **Have each player join with their own pad.** Most co-op games register a new player when they press a button on an unassigned controller.
4. **Pick characters together.** Unlocked characters bring different abilities, so avoid doubling up on the same role if you can help it.
5. **Do one practice arena before committing.** Learn how the chain tugs before you are fighting a boss with it.
6. **Agree on a lead walker and a retreat call.** Two short phrases — "follow me" and "back off" — will save more runs than any build.
7. **Accept the first wipe.** Permadeath is the point. The lesson from run one is usually "stop walking in opposite directions."

## Chain Mechanics: The Real Skill of Chained Beasts Local Coop

The chain is not a cosmetic gimmick. It is a physics object that connects your team, and it can be used deliberately: you can trip enemies with it, drag a partner out of danger, or turn an opponent's charge into a collision with a spike wall. It can also leave four gladiators wrapped around a pillar while a boss winds up a swing.

| Situation | What usually happens | Better play |
|---|---|---|
| Chain snagged on a pillar | Two players lose momentum and eat free hits | The anchor calls one direction; everyone circles the same way |
| Two players sprint apart | The chain snaps taut and both stop dead | Nominate a lead walker and follow their line |
| Enemy near a spike wall or snake pit | Wasted damage on safe ground | Bait, trip, or hurl foes into the hazard instead |
| One player drifting toward a pit | The chain pulls a second player in | Keep slack short near hazards and call the edge out loud |
| Last weapon thrown | Someone is suddenly unarmed mid-fight | Carry a backup or grab arena clutter |
| Boss with a wide sweep | The chain tangles at the worst moment | Spread to max chain length, then collapse together after the attack |

Treat the environment as a fifth party member. Urns, spikes, pits, and walls do more work than most weapons, and a well-aimed throw of a *person* is often funnier and more effective than a sword swing.

## Team Roles That Work in Chained Beasts Local Coop

Random button-mashing gets a group through the first arena and no further. Assigning loose roles gives everyone a job and cuts down on accidental chain pulls.

| Role | Main job | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Sets direction, calls retreats, keeps the chain untangled | One voice prevents the "everyone sprints different ways" freeze |
| Striker | Pressures enemies up close and finishes staggered foes | Keeps damage flowing while others reposition |
| Thrower | Uses urns, thrown weapons, and environment kills | Turns hazards into offense without risking the chain |
| Crowd worker | Plays to the audience and manages coin and upgrades | Crowd favor and gear compound over a run |

These are not rigid classes — anyone can throw an urn — but agreeing on who leads and who handles ranged chaos makes the first ten runs far less frustrating. Rotate roles between sessions so nobody is stuck babysitting the chain every night.

## Tips for Surviving Longer in Chained Beasts Local Coop

Chained Beasts is difficult by design, and the wound system punishes sloppy play across an entire run. Injuries accumulate: a limp that slows you, a missing eye, hallucinations only your character can see. Potions dull the pain but can bring side effects of their own, and surgery is always an option worth weighing.

| Common mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Splitting up to loot | The chain drags players back together at the worst time | Loot as a group, in one direction |
| Ignoring wounds | A limp compounds into a lost run | Treat injuries early, before a boss fight |
| Spending all coin immediately | You miss a weapon that would carry the run | Hold some coin for the mid-run shop |
| Chasing crowd favor alone | The crowd rewards coordinated spectacle | Set up multi-player kills and hazard throws |
| Blaming the chain | It is a tool, not a handicap | Practice dragging and tripping on purpose |

One more tip that sounds trivial but is not: keep the session short. Roguelikes reward repetition, and a group that plays three focused runs will learn faster than a group that grinds six tired ones.

## Chained Beasts Local Coop vs Online Co-op

Both modes are supported, and both are worth using depending on the night.

| Factor | Local co-op | Online co-op |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Instant, face to face, loud | Requires headsets and discipline |
| Latency | None | Depends on connections |
| Screen space | Shared, so less room per player | Full screen per player |
| Setup effort | Controllers, seating, one machine | Invites, accounts, schedules |
| Best for | Parties, first runs, teaching new players | Regular groups spread across cities |

If your group is split, Remote Play Together is the closest thing to Chained Beasts local coop without anyone boarding a train — the host runs the session and guests join in remotely. Just be ready for a slight delay on chain physics, which matters more here than in most co-op games.

## FAQ

**How many players can join Chained Beasts local coop on one screen?**
Up to four. The game supports 1–4 players, and shared/split-screen co-op is an official feature, so a full party can play from a single machine.

**Do I need a controller for Chained Beasts local coop?**
The Steam system requirements state that at least one gamepad is required for same-screen co-op, and the developers recommend playing with a controller overall. For a smooth four-player session, plan on one pad per player.

**Can I play Chained Beasts local coop with friends who are not in the room?**
Yes. Remote Play Together lets a host run a local session and invite Steam friends, and the game also offers standard online co-op plus cross-platform multiplayer.

**Is Chained Beasts local coop good for casual groups?**
It is friendly to drop-in players because the premise is instantly clear — you are chained to your friends and must fight together — but the difficulty is real. Expect a few wipes while your group learns to move as one, and treat those losses as the tutorial.
