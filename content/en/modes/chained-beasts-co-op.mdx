The arena gates open, four gladiators are shackled together, and within ten seconds somebody has been swung into a spike wall. That is the core promise of **Chained Beasts co op**: a top-down roguelike where your friends are simultaneously your best weapon and your worst liability. Released on October 15, 2026 by Featherweight Games, the game asks one question — can you keep a chain coordinated long enough to win the crowd and buy your freedom?

If you have played co-op roguelikes before, the twist here is physical. A Chained Beasts co op run does not just drop four players into the same arena; it ties them together with a physics-driven chain, so every dodge, charge, and weapon throw drags somebody else along. Learning to move as one unit is the entire skill curve. That is also why the game's Very Positive Steam rating — 83% of 655 user reviews at the time of writing — comes bundled with a lot of stories about accidental friendly fire.

## Chained Beasts Co Op Modes: Every Way to Play

The game supports up to four players and does not force a single style of play. Whether you want a full online party, a couch full of controllers, or a solo run to learn the arena before inviting anyone, the options cover it. Here is how they compare.

| Mode | Players | What you need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 1 | Keyboard and mouse or a controller | Learning chain physics without blaming anyone else |
| Online co-op | 2–4 | Internet connection and friends on your list | Long-distance groups and full four-player chaos |
| Shared/split-screen co-op | 2–4 | At least one gamepad for the same-screen setup | Couch sessions and same-room shouting |
| Cross-platform multiplayer | 2–4 | Supported platforms on both ends | Mixed groups that are not all on PC |
| Remote Play Together | 2–4 | A host who owns the game | Inviting a friend who has not bought a copy yet |

According to [the official Chained Beasts Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3569420/Chained_Beasts/), the game ships with single-player, online co-op, shared/split-screen co-op, cross-platform multiplayer, and Remote Play Together support. That means a Chained Beasts co op night rarely falls apart over a platform mismatch. A free demo is also available, which is the cheapest possible way to find out whether your group can survive being tied together before anyone spends money.

A few practical notes before you pick a mode:

- **Two players is the calmest entry point.** Fewer links means fewer tangles, and it is easier to hear one person call a direction than three.
- **Four players is the funniest and the hardest.** More bodies mean more momentum, more collisions, and more chances to drag a teammate into a pit of snakes.
- **Solo is not a downgrade.** It is the fastest way to learn enemy tells and arena layouts before you add other people's bad decisions to the mix.
- **Use the demo for a group test.** If your friends cannot coordinate for ten minutes, they will not coordinate through a boss.

## Chain Mechanics: How to Move as One Unit

Every player in a Chained Beasts co op run is physically linked, and the link is not decorative. Charging, dodging, and throwing weapons all transfer momentum through the chain. When the chain goes taut, it yanks. When it goes slack, it snags on pillars and urns. Good groups treat the chain as a shared resource rather than an obstacle.

### The tension problem

A slack chain is a snagged chain. If everyone wanders, the link catches on scenery and stalls the whole group at the worst possible moment. The fix is boring but effective: pick one player as the anchor, have them set the pace, and let everyone else stay roughly one body-length behind. Predictable spacing keeps the chain taut enough to whip and loose enough to dodge.

### Using the chain offensively

The chain is a weapon. A sharp turn from the anchor whips the chain across enemy legs and trips them, which is exactly the kind of crowd-pleasing move that earns favor with the arena audience. Coordinated charges can also shove enemies into spike walls or off ledges. The catch is that every offensive use of the chain moves your teammates too, so it has to be called out loud before it happens, not after.

### Rescuing a tangled teammate

Tangled players are a when, not an if. The instinct is to pull harder, which usually wraps the group tighter around whatever they are stuck on. Instead, stop moving, let the chain settle for a beat, and have one player walk a slow circle in the opposite direction of the tangle. Panic costs more runs than bosses do.

| Situation | Bad instinct | Better play |
|---|---|---|
| Boss winds up a charge | Dodge away from the chain | Dodge sideways so the chain cuts across the boss's path |
| You want to throw a weapon | Throw mid-sprint | Call the throw, plant, then release so the chain stays controlled |
| The group is stuck on a pillar | Everyone pulls in a different direction | One player anchors while the others circle the same way |
| A teammate is nearly dead | Sprint to the far side of the arena | Stay mid-range so potions and resupply are reachable |
| A boss is staggered | Everyone piles in from one side | Split angles so the chain can trip the boss on the swing |

## Team Roles That Keep a Chain Alive

Random groups usually lose because nobody agreed on a job. Assigning loose roles before the first fight makes a Chained Beasts co op session dramatically smoother, and it costs about thirty seconds of conversation.

| Role | Main job | Chain position | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Sets direction, calls the count, controls pace | Front | Keeps the chain taut and the group predictable |
| Bruiser | Crowd control, grapples, throwing enemies | Middle | Borrows momentum from the anchor to slam into packs |
| Skirmisher | Thrown weapons, urns, ranged pressure | Middle to back | Punishes enemies while the chain holds the line |
| Surgeon | Potions, wound triage, resource tracking | Back | Keeps the run alive once injuries start stacking |

In a two-player Chained Beasts co op run, the anchor-and-skirmisher split works best because both players can see the same fight. Add a bruiser at three players, and a dedicated support at four. Community reports from early launch groups consistently point to the same failure pattern: four players all trying to be the anchor, nobody calling directions, and a wipe inside the first arena.

A short pre-run checklist helps:

1. Name an anchor and let them lead the first fight.
2. Agree on one direction to circle when someone gets tangled.
3. Decide who carries healing and who carries throwing weapons.
4. Set a rule that offensive chain pulls get called before they happen.

## Bosses, Wounds, and Damage Control

Fame is the difficulty dial. As your crowd favor rises, the arena sends tougher bosses — veteran gladiators armed with every advantage the game can hand them. The store page is blunt about it: the deck is stacked against you, and victory only moves you one step closer to Rome.

Wounds are the other half of the pressure. Each day in the arena leaves lasting damage, and the game leans into dark comedy about it. A painful limp, a missing left eye, hallucinations only one player can see — all of it persists between fights. Potions relieve pain but can inflict worse afflictions, and surgery is presented as the risky alternative.

| Affliction | How the game describes it | Practical workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Limp | Slower, painful movement | Put that player at the back of the chain and let the anchor set pace |
| Missing eye | Blinded on one side | Pair them with a teammate who calls out flank attacks |
| Hallucinations | Seeing enemies nobody else can see | Trust voice callouts over what is on your own screen |
| Potion side effects | New afflictions after treatment | Only drink when the wound is worse than the possible side effect |
| Surgery | A heavier, riskier fix | Save it for downtime between fights rather than mid-brawl |

Boss preparation, in order of impact:

- **Learn the arena before the boss.** Environmental kills work on bosses far more often than raw damage does.
- **Bank crowd favor early.** A crowd that likes you is a resource you can lean on in later fights.
- **Keep one throwing weapon in reserve.** Ranged finishers are worth more than a slightly better sword.
- **Never enter a boss fight with an untreated limp.** Mobility is your chain's steering wheel.

## Progression, Builds, and Setup for Chained Beasts Co Op

Between runs, the game layers on the usual roguelike scaffolding: skills to learn, equipment to buy, new characters with unique abilities to unlock, and crowd favor to farm. The store page notes that each run pushes you down a different path, so builds should flex rather than lock in.

| Where to spend early | Why it pays off | When to skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Skills that improve chain control | Fixes the most common wipe cause | Skip if your group already coordinates well |
| Weapons with throw value | Gives the skirmisher real ranged pressure | Skip if nobody can aim under pressure |
| Character unlocks | Adds abilities that change team composition | Skip until you have cleared a few arenas |

## FAQ

**Is Chained Beasts co op online only?**
No. It supports online co-op, shared/split-screen co-op, cross-platform multiplayer, Remote Play Together, and full single-player. A Chained Beasts co op session can happen on one couch, across two continents, or with a friend who does not own the game via Remote Play Together.

**How many players can join a run?**
Between one and four. The game scales from a solo gladiator to a four-player chain, and the difficulty curve shifts accordingly. Four players is the most chaotic and generally the hardest to coordinate.

**Do all players need a controller?**
Not all, but same-screen co-op requires at least one gamepad, and the developers recommend controllers overall. If your group is split between input devices, test a demo session before committing to a long run.

**Is Chained Beasts co op beginner friendly?**
It is friendly in structure and unforgiving in execution. The free demo, solo mode, and 12 supported languages lower the entry barrier, while permanent death and Souls-like combat tags set the ceiling. Expect a few wipes before your chain stops snagging on pillars.

The short version: Chained Beasts co op rewards groups that talk, plan a direction, and resist the urge to yank. Pick an anchor, keep the chain taut, call your pulls, and treat every wound as a new constraint rather than a run-ending disaster. Do that, and the crowd — and eventually the gates to Rome — will open for you.
