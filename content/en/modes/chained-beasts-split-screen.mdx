## How Chained Beasts Split Screen Co-op Works

Chained Beasts is a 1–4 player co-op gladiator roguelike from Featherweight Games. You and your teammates are physically chained together in the arena, and that design choice shapes movement, positioning, revives, and environmental kills. The game launched on October 15, 2026, and its [Chained Beasts’ Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3569420/Chained_Beasts/) lists Shared/Split Screen Co-op and Shared/Split Screen among its features, along with Local Co-Op and 4 Player Local tags.

The Chained Beasts split screen option is best understood as a local-play feature for the same co-op game, not a separate campaign. Steam also lists Online Co-op, Cross-Platform Multiplayer, and Remote Play Together, so groups can connect in several ways. The store page does not publish a separate split-screen ruleset, so expect the same core loop: survive the arena, win the crowd, manage injuries, and push toward freedom.

One practical detail matters more than any marketing label: the system requirements state that at least one gamepad is required for same-screen co-op. If you are planning a four-player couch session, do not assume four keyboards will work. Check your controller situation before friends arrive, and keep a spare cable or charged pad handy.

## Practical Tips for Chained Beasts Split Screen Sessions

Chained Beasts split screen amplifies the game’s best and worst tendencies. The chain is a tool, but it is also a leash: players who sprint in different directions will tangle around pillars, drag each other into hazards, or waste a boss window. The table below turns common local-play problems into simple team calls.

| Situation | Risky move | Better team call |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow doorway | Everyone pushes through at once | “Single file—anchor follows” |
| Boss windup | Scatter in four directions | “Stack left, dodge together” |
| Downed ally | One player sprints across the arena | “Nearest two peel, third holds the chain” |
| Crowd bonus | Ignore the audience | “Taunt on the safe side, then reset” |
| Weapon throw | Throw with no backup | “Call the throw, cover the recovery” |

Assign a caller. In a four-player local session, one person should make the big calls—retreat, focus the boss, use the environment, or rescue a teammate. This is not about bossing people around; it is about reducing the number of conflicting directions the chain has to survive.

Keep slack in the chain. Tight formations are useful in corridors, but open arenas reward a little spacing. If everyone crowds the same enemy, one sweep can hit the whole team. Spread just enough to avoid shared damage while staying close enough to help.

Use the arena as a weapon. The game encourages creative environment kills: urns, spikes, pits, and other hazards can do work that your weapons cannot. In local play, call out the hazard before you drag a boss toward it so a teammate does not get pulled in too.

Manage wounds as a team. Injuries are part of the run, and a player with a limp or a missing eye is not useless—but they may need a different role. Let the wounded player handle crowd control or ranged pressure while healthier teammates take the front line.

## Troubleshooting and Performance Notes

Most Chained Beasts split screen problems are ordinary local-co-op issues rather than game-breaking bugs. Work through the simple causes first: controller pairing, display settings, USB ports, and background load. If you are playing through Remote Play Together, test your internet connection before blaming the game.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Second controller not detected | Pairing or port issue | Re-pair the pad, try another USB port, and test it in the demo |
| Frame rate drops with four players | Hardware load or high settings | Lower graphics settings, close background apps, and test with fewer players |
| Players cannot see their character | Display cropping or camera confusion | Check TV overscan/game mode and give each player a distinct position callout |
| Remote friend cannot join | Steam Remote Play or network issue | Confirm the host has a stable connection and the invite is accepted |
| Audio callouts get lost | Shared speakers or overlapping chat | Use push-to-talk, shorten calls, or route chat through a headset |

Steam Deck compatibility is listed as Unknown at the time of writing, so do not assume a smooth handheld local session. If you plan to play on Deck or a low-power laptop, try the demo first. The same advice applies to cross-platform groups: the feature is listed, but platform-specific details can change, so confirm before you schedule a session.
